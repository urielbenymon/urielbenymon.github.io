I am an undergraduate student at Columbia University's School of General Studies, studying Information Science with a minor in Mathematics. My research interests lie at the intersection of natural language processing, interpretable machine learning, and language model evaluation.

I am particularly interested in how language models acquire and represent linguistic knowledge, how their learning processes compare with those of human learners, and how we can better understand and evaluate their behavior. My current interests include multilingual language models, interpretability, and evaluation robustness.

My research spans language model evaluation, machine learning applications in healthcare, and database systems. My work has been accepted to the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Linguistic Principles for Foundation Models (LP4FM) and the Machine Learning for Health (ML4H) 2026 Findings Track. I have also participated in undergraduate research through Columbia's Bancroft Research Fellowship and RISE program.

Alongside research, I have served as a Teaching Assistant for Introduction to Computer Science and Programming in Java and a Course Assistant for Data Structures at Columbia University. Previously, I worked as a peer tutor at Spartanburg Community College.

I welcome opportunities to connect with researchers working in natural language processing, machine learning, and related areas.
